Novartis CEO says AI will shave four years off drug development, not cure all disease

Vas Narasimhan, CEO of Swiss drugmaker Novartis and an Anthropic board member, is pushing back on expectations that AI could cure cancer in five to 10 years. That puts him at odds with Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and chemistry Nobel laureate Demis Hassabis, who founded DeepMind and its drug discovery spinoff Isomorphic. Around Anthropic's IPO, Amodei suggested that cancer, Alzheimer's, heart disease, and mental illness could be cured in under a decade, while Hassabis said last year that AI could defeat all diseases within 10 years.

According to the Financial Times, Narasimhan points to a limited understanding of human biology, which is why lengthy animal and human trials will still be needed. His forecast is more cautious: within a decade, he expects AI to cut average drug development only from 12 years to eight, with the early phase before human trials alone accounting for up to two years of that. He also expects success rates in clinical development to rise from 8 percent to 12 percent.

Anthropic is investing in its own drug research, acquired biotech startup Coefficient Bio, and is building a biology lab. Isomorphic raised $5 billion last week and is working with pharmaceutical companies including Novartis on new drugs. Narasimhan sees AI's real benefit as "somewhere in the middle" between ambitious AI forecasts and a skeptical pharma industry.
