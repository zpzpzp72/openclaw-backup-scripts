Yes, 17m lines of Lean is a lot

2026-11-22

AI disclosure: I wrote all text here, but used Agent Duel (an open source battle system on top of OpenCode) to research sources of Lean proofs and generate charts.

The recent proof of the Riemann hypothesis came with a statistic attached: the proof is 17 million lines of Lean. Beyond the philosophical questions of what it means to have a proof longer than any human could comprehend, I’m left with a simpler question: is that as large as it sounds?

To investigate, I hunted for collections of Lean proofs online. I found two and charted the lengths of their proofs.

(All charts have a log x axis, which makes 17m lines look a lot closer to 1m and 100k than it really is.)

Famous theorems in Mathlib

Mathlib maintains a collection of famous proofs. There are over 1k but only 259 have Lean associated, which I chart here.

These are all human generated, and as we can see, they come nowhere close to 17m.

Every Lean package on Reservoir

Reservoir indexes every published Lean package, 938 of which have Lean code on GitHub. This one has a lot more data, including AI assisted proofs. We even have a few that are over 16m!:

  1. 88m: Qiuzhen-CFSG/CFSG this appears to be an AI built proof that is still a work in progress.

  2. 36m: CBirkbeck/LeanBridge, a project linking the LMFDB database of number theory objects to Mathlib. This isn’t really a proof: about 98% of its Lean is files generated by scripts that check the database’s numbers one by one. The hand-written part is closer to 500k lines.

  3. 27m: anthropics/fermats-last-theorem is another Anthropic special: a complete proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem, published on GitHub in September. Its README says “the Lean sources were produced by AI agents building on human-written open-source Lean”, including the human FLT project led by Kevin Buzzard, which is about 60k lines and still in progress. The AI version is roughly 450 times longer.

So yes, 17m lines is a lot.
